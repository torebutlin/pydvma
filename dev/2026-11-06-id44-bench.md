# iD44 acoustics round: bench notes (2026-11-06)

## Long recordings

Task 11 of `dev/plans/2026-11-06-id44-acoustics-plan.md`. Tore's first use
of the iD44 is 1–5 minute ambient recordings of four microphones at 48 kHz:
14.4 M samples × 4 channels, 461 MB of float64 time data per capture plus a
115 MB float64 time axis. The spec: such a recording must capture, display,
compute PSD and spectrogram (STFT), save, reload and survive tab-close
restore through `pydvma-serve` with the native engine, with no data loss
and no silent fallback.

Harness: `dev/long_capture_check.py` (mock serve on port 8771; no audio
device is opened). It drives the path the app takes, hop by hop: a `/ws`
capture, then over `/engine` the journal's pending capture, the app's
default PSD (Hann, 10 frames) and STFT (nFFT 512, channel 0) requests with
the whole capture, the capture posted and read back as the session
document, and Save/Load through the working folder. One row per step with
the wall time, the bytes moved and the peak RSS of the serve process and of
its `/engine` worker subprocess. The mock runs in real time; its data are
deterministic 0.1 V sines, which deflate to about half, so the harness also
measures, in its own process, a noise DataSet of the same shape.

Mac (M-series, 48 GB), 2026-11-06, base `e7657ca`.

Two more findings from the baseline, both on every large transfer:

- **permessage-deflate.** `websockets` negotiates it with any client that
  offers it (browsers do), and then deflates every outbound frame and
  inflates every inbound one on the serve event loop. Measured with a
  200 MB `journal_set`/`journal_get`: 4.72 / 4.59 s with it, 0.29 / 0.11 s
  without. That is the ~6 s on every 268 MB row above, and for its
  duration the live scope, `/ws` and every other `/engine` connection
  stall.
- **Deflating the container.** `save_bytes` of 518 MB of noise took 12.5 s
  to save 12 %; in the browser (node, same fflate code), `writeDvma` of a
  4 ch × 300 s noise session took 14.5 s on the main thread, on every
  autosave (2 s after each change), and reading a bridged capture unzipped
  it twice (6.5 s).
